{format_rules}
- Annotate only the input sentence. The guidance JSON string supplies the sentence's language tag and the surrounding source paragraph as context; context may resolve who or what a mention refers to, but never copy a span from the context and never translate, normalize, or repair the input.
- Emit only spans that carry one of the allowed tags. Never emit an element whose type is O, none, other, or any name outside the allowed list: a surface that is not PII is simply omitted from the array.
- Every emitted surface must be one contiguous substring copied character-for-character, including whitespace and punctuation inside the span. Return annotations in increasing start-position order, with the longer span first when starts are equal, one element per distinct occurrence and tag. A reference may properly contain independently meaningful non-reference spans, with different intervals; preserve both. Do not emit crossing spans, overlapping references, or overlapping non-reference spans.
- The allowed-tag definitions are the annotation scope. Label every occurrence that satisfies one, including repeated mentions and public or historical people, organizations, facilities, places, monetary amounts, and demographic attributes; do not apply a separate legal or privacy threshold.
- Use the mention's function in context. A person-looking string used as a company, venue, product, hospital, university, fictional or media title, or other non-person entity is not a person name.
- person_name is one maximal span: every component of the name together with an honorific written directly before or after it. An honorific is a closed-class form of address or academic courtesy title, such as Mr., Mrs., Ms., Dr., Prof., Frau, Herr, Mme, Sir, さん, 様, or 씨. Never split one person's name into component spans, and never also emit the name without its title as a second person_name. A job title, office, rank, or profession beside the name is not part of it, however short: "Senator", "President", "Chief Minister", "former minister", and a position word written after a name all stay outside ("Senator Ana Ruiz", "former minister Rui Tanaka", "the doctor Irene Vos"). Label such an apposed descriptor, or a role noun ("the victim Tomas Varga"), as a separate adjacent span as described for person_reference.
- person_reference is a pronoun, possessive, relative pronoun, nominal, or role phrase that the sentence or supplied context grounds to a specific person or definite group of people: "she", "who", "the applicant", "his mother", "the patient", "their lawyers", "our legal team's director". Include the full referential phrase with its article, determiner, or possessive; do not also emit a contained pronoun or determiner as a second reference. A proper name, pseudonym, recognizable alias, or anonymized initial functioning as a name is person_name, never person_reference. A reference never contains the name of the same person it refers to: when a role or relational phrase stands beside a person's name and denotes that same person, label the phrase and the name as separate adjacent spans, never one span covering both. The phrase is person_reference when it is a role or relation ("his daughter" in "his daughter Mira", "the victim" in "the victim Tomas Varga") and demographic_attribute when it only states a profession, office, or nationality of the named person ("grocer" in "the grocer Yusuf", "foreign minister" in "the foreign minister Elena Moravec"; its span excludes the article). A role or profession phrase with no name beside it that itself refers to a specific person is person_reference ("the journalist" in "the paper's eager journalist wrote"). A reference may contain the name of a different person through whom it identifies its referent: "John's father" and "Ronaldo's girlfriend" are each one person_reference, with "John" and "Ronaldo" also labeled person_name. A reference is of unspecified count: a plural or counted phrase grounded to a specific group is one person_reference ("her two nephews", "the four siblings", "the two Kennedy sisters"), and a descriptor inside the phrase stays inside it ("a 49-year-old man", "the daughter, aged 50"). Generic, hypothetical, universally quantified, or attributive mentions remain untagged, whether singular or plural ("a patient must", "patients generally", "Republicans", "children with autism").
- organization_reference is a pronoun, possessive, relative pronoun, anaphoric, or nominal mention grounded to a specific organization or definite institutional group but that is not its name: "it" referring to a named company, "the company", "the domestic courts", "the ministry", "our legal team". Include the full referential phrase with its article or determiner; do not also emit a contained pronoun or determiner as a second reference. A proper institutional name, acronym, alias, conventional official short name, or explicitly declared short name is organization, never organization_reference. A common institutional noun used attributively or in a generic proposition remains untagged.
- A named body counts as an organization even when it exists only for a limited time or a single purpose: a committee, subcommittee, working group, task force, panel, commission of inquiry, board, delegation, organising committee, jury, funded project consortium, or the standing programme or plan that such a body runs. What makes it an organization is that people constitute it and act through it, not that it is permanent. Examples of the borderline: "the Election Management Committee", "the WeSTEMEU project", "the National Reform Programme", "the Common Strategic Framework", "Nasjonal Transportplan", together with their declared acronyms. Label the body's name; do not also label the document, report, or law it issues.
- A product, device model, application, service, publication, album, film, tour, standard, or statute is not an organization and is not a person. Leave it untagged unless a different allowed tag genuinely fits. "WonderSwan Color", "Xperia XZs", "Gmail", "Chocolate Slim", "Journal of the Asiatic Society", and "Employment Standards Act" all take no span, even when the surrounding sentence is about the company that makes or publishes them. Label that company separately when its own name appears.
- Label every occurrence of the same entity in the input, including ones that differ only by inflection, an attached case or possessive suffix, a clitic, capitalisation, or an orthographic variant of the same script. If a company name appears three times in one sentence and one of them is spelled differently, all three still get the same tag. A repeated mention is never dropped for being repeated.
- These reference rules apply to equivalents in the input language, including standalone pronouns and possessives. A reference phrase is headed by a word denoting the person, group, or organization. When a possessive modifies a noun denoting something else, such as an object, document, plan, body part, or event, only the possessive is the reference: "his" in "his passport", "its" in "its plans", "sein" in "sein Reisepass". Tag a pronoun, relative pronoun, or demonstrative only after identifying its antecedent and confirming that it is a specific person, definite group of people, organization, or definite institutional group; one that points to a thing, place, document, recording, event, or idea stays untagged. Dummy/expletive pronouns ("it" in "it is raining"), references to objects or propositions, and unresolved or generic pronouns remain untagged. Context can ground a referent without giving its name; do not invent a span for an omitted subject.

Religion, ethnicity, political opinion, trade-union membership, and sexual orientation of a specific person are protected_attribute.
- government_id is limited to a personal identifier issued by a government or state register. A customer, patient, employee, policy, case, order, or other organization-issued number is record_identifier; a bank account or wallet is bank_account_number; a card number is payment_card_data.
- Identifier, contact, account and credential tags require the actual value to be written in the input. A field name, category word, document type, form label, or instruction to enter a value is untagged even when it refers to a specific person's data. Thus "enter your password", "forgot my username", "new PIN", "account number" and "UDID" have no identifier or credential span by themselves. - When a written value is explicitly used as a password, access code, secret key or authentication token, label that value credential even if its characters look like a URL, email address, username or ordinary name. The stated use takes precedence over the string's appearance; this does not make the surrounding field name a credential.
- A device product/model name and a software version are not device_identifier or ip_address. Use their stated function in context even if a version looks like an IP address. A written hostname that identifies a particular device can be device_identifier.
- A literal account handle, forum username, or poster identity is username rather than person_name. Separately label a person's written name when the text supplies it.
- A monetary amount includes its currency unit inside one span. A non-currency count, percentage, or measure that is quasi-identifying in context is quantity; a generic elapsed duration is untagged.
- Generic, class-level, and predicative nominals are untagged. An indefinite but specific introduction can be a reference. When a reference phrase contains an independently meaningful organization name, person name, age, or other non-reference entity that is not the referent itself, emit the full reference and the contained entity with their exact original boundaries. For example, "Google's lawyer" contains a person_reference over the whole phrase and organization over "Google", and "the UMP's nominating committee" contains an organization_reference over the whole phrase and organization over "UMP". Do not shorten the reference to avoid nesting; flattening is a separate downstream operation. Only a name or other non-reference span may sit inside a reference; a reference inside another reference is never emitted ("the bank" in "a branch of the bank" stays inside the one organization_reference). A name never receives a reference span over itself: a name carrying an attached case, possessive, or topic suffix or particle is only its name span, and a descriptor apposed to a name of the same entity is a separate adjacent span, not a wrapper.
- An organization name excludes a leading article or generic head noun unless that text is part of the proper name. A named facility containing a place name is one organization or location span; do not also label the place inside it.
- Relative or deictic time ("the same day", "last spring") and years embedded in statute, standard, reporter, or series names are untagged. A calendar date with an attached clock time is one date span.
- An amount whose currency unit is missing or corrupted is not monetary_amount. A product model number, standard number, or company discount code is untagged unless it identifies a person's record or is a secret.
- A health fact or personal attribute is labeled only when stated about a specific person or definite group, not in generic advice, product copy, or job requirements.
- Span boundaries exclude surrounding punctuation, prepositions, and case-marking function words that are separate tokens.
- Copy every span exactly as it is written in the input, never in a dictionary, nominative, corrected, or completed form, and never restore letters the text does not contain. When a suffix, particle, or prefix is attached to a word inside a span: if the unchanged written form of the entity still appears in the text, end or start the span at that form and leave the affix out; if attaching the affix rewrote the end of the word, the span is the whole inflected word as written.
